Irregularities worth a human eye

  • The MTA fare is $3, not $2.90. Older guides and cached pages still quote $2.90. The official fares page read on 25 Nov 2026 states $3 for subway and local bus, and $7.25 for express bus. Any budget built on $2.90 is out of date.
  • Booking.com inflated then discounted. Arlo NoMad displayed “$1,989 → $1,214, 39% off”, with the discount attributed to a Black Friday promotion plus a payment-method adjustment. The struck-through figure is a reference price, not a price anyone pays.
  • Two hotels add a mandatory “resort fee” inside the quoted total — $35/night at 11 Howard, $37/night at Arlo NoMad, $57/night at The Beekman. It is already inside the pre-tax total, which surprises people comparing nightly rates.
  • The Beekman’s tax stack differs: a $6 destination tax and a $2 “TAX” line on top of the 14.75% city tax, plus a $2 tourism fee. It is the only shortlisted hotel with that combination.
  • MICHELIN lists a restaurant inside a hotel. Le Coucou ★ is shown by the guide as the restaurant of 11 Howard — a genuine, verifiable concentration of starred dining, and the strongest proximity evidence on the site.

What was explicitly not done

  • No value was inferred from a search-result snippet alone. Snippets were used only to locate a page, and the page was then read.
  • No restaurant was assumed open because it “usually” opens — only verified weekday hours are used for scheduling.
  • No price was averaged, smoothed or rounded into a range that hides its source.
  • No social handle was guessed from a name or a search suggestion.
  • No travel time was estimated from distance: a one-hour claim needs a routing source.
