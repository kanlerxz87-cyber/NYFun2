Irregularities requiring integration review

  • Conflicting official menu files: Le Bernardin’s older-looking PDF displays $218 per person, while its accessible dinner page displays $225 and links a different PDF. Use the latter only as the currently displayed page, not a guaranteed future price; reconfirm directly before booking.
  • Official-source requirement: the main dashboard displays third-party hotel rate snapshots; the repository itself identifies these as aggregator rates rather than hotel quotes. They do not satisfy the requested official-source-only price standard. Recheck the actual hotel booking engine for each exact room, stay and cancellation policy before treating a price as verified. Review hotel table.
  • Room evidence: some hotel rows show a room and bathroom while simultaneously flagging that configuration as unverified (for example 11 Howard). Do not treat these as qualifying until corroborated by the property itself. Review hotel records.
  • Restaurant menu gap: the main dataset leaves couple prices blank, although official menu prices above were accessible. The integration owner should verify and incorporate explicitly labelled subtotals without presenting them as all-in totals. Review restaurant data.
  • Automated check scope: the existing verifier checks local links and HTTPS-shaped source fields; it does not fetch sources, verify claims, validate social reviews, or test all datasets and pages in the namespaced kits. Its passing output is not proof of line-by-line verification. Review verifier.
  • Scope: this is a targeted audit, not a full line-by-line re-verification of the repository. Hours, social links, Michelin status, fares, lodging prices and route times remain subject to independent verification.
