Two kinds of listing A 3★ Michelin badge means actual stars awarded by the Guide. A Guide listed or Bib Gourmand badge means the restaurant is in the MICHELIN Guide without a star: the Guide recommends it, but it holds no star. New York also lists 224 restaurants with no distinction at all.

How cost for two is shown MICHELIN publishes a price band - $$$$ is the top band, $ the lowest - not a menu price. The band is an official MICHELIN field and is listed per restaurant. Dollar amounts are deliberately NOT derived from it: a $$$$ dinner for two is not known to be any specific number, and inventing one would break the rule that nothing is estimated. Where a restaurant publishes its own menu price, it appears as text instead.

The count does not quite reconcile The live all-starred filter reports 5 / 14 / 50 = 69. The official 2025 ceremony article names 5 / 15 / 52. The two extra rows are in the two- and one-star tiers. This site follows the live filter; the discrepancy is logged as flag F-01 rather than smoothed over.

Cost for two, honestly

Prices are not on the MICHELIN pages MICHELIN publishes a price tier (e.g. $$$$) but not the menu price. Tasting-menu prices for these restaurants are therefore left blank rather than estimated, and the Open Questions page lists this as the single biggest missing input for budgeting.

Roster notes

  • Michelin's live 'all starred' filter reports 69 starred (5 / 14 / 50) and 76 Bib Gourmand, but the official 18 Nov 2025 ceremony article lists 5 / 15 / 52. The difference appears in the two-star tier (article has 15; filter 14) and one-star tier (article 52; filter 50). Rows above follow the live filter/starred pages (the current public selection).
