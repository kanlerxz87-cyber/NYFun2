Starred, Bib Gourmand, or merely listed — kept apart

The brief asks explicitly whether a restaurant holds a MICHELIN star or is only listed in the guide. This page keeps those categories separate, states the official counter for each, and marks every row that is catalogued here versus merely counted.

69 restaurants carry a star in New York City and surroundings (5 + 14 + 50); 76 more hold a Bib Gourmand, and 224 are listed with no award at all. The distinction between “has a star” and “is in the guide” is the single most important column on this site.

What “listed but not starred” means here

The guide’s 224 “Selected” restaurants and 76 Bib Gourmand restaurants have no star. They are good restaurants recognised by the guide, and they are usually far cheaper. Two examples seen while reading the official list — Oriana (selected, American, $$$) and Mūje (selected, Asian, $$$$) — are guide-listed, not starred. If the goal is a memorable meal rather than a star count, the Bib Gourmand list is where the value is, and it is entirely un-catalogued here yet.

Bib Gourmand definition as published by MICHELIN: “good quality, good value cooking”.
