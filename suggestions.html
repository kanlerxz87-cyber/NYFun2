Do S10, S5, S2 only: fix trip length, price three alternative weeks, capture four tasting-menu prices. Everything else stays as-is.

You end up able to choose a hotel, a week and a dinner budget. Proximity and social coverage stay unverified and visibly flagged.

Suggested order of work

  1. S10 first. Deciding 7 vs 14 nights invalidates or validates every number on the site — it is a single answer with a large blast radius.
  2. Then S5. Four more windows × four hotels turns “we priced one week” into “we found the cheapest week”, which is literally what the brief asks.
  3. Then S2. Menu prices are the only genuinely large unknown left in the food line.
  4. Then S1 + S3 together. A routing API is only worth wiring once the full restaurant list exists; doing S1 against 4 verified venues would be misleading.
  5. S6, S7, S8 are polish. Valuable, but they do not change a decision.
  6. S4 and S9 are governance. Adopt them as rules so future passes cannot quietly invent a handle or an opening time.

Rules this site has been holding itself to

What counts as verified

A value is verified only when an official page — or a booking-partner page the venue itself links to — states it, and that page was read during the session. The row then links to it.

What happens when it cannot be

The cell stays empty and gets a flag ID. There is no fallback to “roughly”, “usually” or “approximately” for a fact. Arithmetic the site performs itself is labelled as an estimate and shows the formula.
