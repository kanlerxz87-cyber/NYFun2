Planning logic

Itinerary & the one-hour travel goal

The request asks to keep activities within one hour of the hotel by subway or bus. Rather than invent door-to-door times, this page shows the verified inputs and the exact step needed to turn them into a route-checked timetable.

What is verified

  • Official addresses for 13 starred restaurants and 12 attractions (see their tables).
  • Published opening days and hours for the majority of those rows.
  • The MTA's official subway and bus maps ↗, which identify the lines serving each area.
  • Live Google Maps directions links embedded in every restaurant and activity row, using the hotel you select as origin.

What is not verified

No official source published a door-to-door travel time from a chosen hotel to every activity for the actual February 2027 dates and times. No such times are asserted here. Consequently hotel ranking by "count of Michelin restaurants within one hour" is marked as a decision blocker (Q5), not presented as a finished score.

Suggested structure (not a confirmed timetable)

  1. Morning blockA museum or outdoor landmark with published morning hours — e.g. Central Park, The Met (opens 10:00), MoMA (opens 10:30).
  2. Lunch / early afternoonUse a venue near the morning block. The High Line (07:00–20:00 in winter) and Whitney (10:30–18:00 except Tuesday) pair naturally.
  3. Evening blockStarred restaurants with published dinner service. Check the exact day: several close Monday or Tuesday.
  4. BufferAdd transfer time from a real routing result before committing to a reservation.

How to finish this page properly

For each shortlisted hotel, route to each shortlisted restaurant and attraction at the intended departure time, record the minutes and the URL, then keep only pairs at 60 minutes or less. That dataset — not an estimate — is what should drive the hotel ranking and the final day-by-day plan.
